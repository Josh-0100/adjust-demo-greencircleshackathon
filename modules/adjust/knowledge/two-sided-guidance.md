# ADJUST two-sided flow: official guidance check

Checked **16 October 2026**, using the published primary pages linked below. This is an editorial source check for a fictional demonstration, not an eligibility decision, funding award or legal assessment. Facts in this record must retain their qualifications when used in the product.

## Access to Work

[DWP employer factsheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-work-guide-for-employers/access-to-work-factsheet-for-employers), sections “What Access to Work will not pay for”, “How much will this cost me?” and “What will my share of the costs be”:

- Access to Work can support a person's own disability or health condition at work. Caring alone does not establish eligibility.
- It does **not** pay for the reasonable adjustments an employer must legally make. Do not delay those adjustments while seeking funding.
- Mandatory sharing generally concerns approved special aids/equipment or premises/equipment adaptations when the employee has been in the job more than six weeks at application. The factsheet describes exemptions and possible funding up to 100% for some support; these are not promises of an award.
- Support that benefits the business generally may require an additional employer contribution.

[DWP staff guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-work-staff-guide/access-to-work-staff-guide), “Cost sharing”, especially “Cost share applies” and “Calculating the threshold and ceiling”, publishes these categories:

| Business size selected in ADJUST | Published staff-guide position |
| --- | --- |
| Under 50 employees | Micro/small categories are marked “Nil cost share”. Confirm the award and any other contribution with Access to Work. |
| 50–249 employees | Employer threshold £500; where sharing applies, employer meets that threshold plus 20% of the next £10,000 of approved shared costs. |
| 250+ employees | Employer threshold £1,000; the same 20% sharing applies to the next £10,000 of approved shared costs. |

**Source discrepancy:** the public factsheet describes under-50 employers as having a *nil threshold*, rather than explicitly no cost share. It also describes the ceiling as costs “between” the threshold and £10,000, whereas staff-guide worked examples deduct the threshold before applying a £10,000 ceiling. Its largest band says “Over 250”; the staff guide says “250+”. The table above follows the explicit staff-guide categories/examples. Show this as indicative guidance with confirmation required; do not calculate or promise a grant from employer size. The employer's private comparison should deduct only an amount they enter as confirmed funding, defaulting to zero. An award can include other conditions, exemptions or contributions.

## Carers' rights and support

**Flexible working:** [GOV.UK, Flexible working: overview](https://www.gov.uk/flexible-working) confirms employees may make a statutory request from their first day. This is a right to **request** changes to hours, timing, days or location, rather than a guarantee that a requested arrangement will be agreed. Employers must handle requests reasonably and can refuse for a valid business reason. Use “Request flexible working from day one”.

**Unpaid Carer's Leave:** [GOV.UK, Unpaid carer's leave](https://www.gov.uk/carers-leave) confirms a day-one employee entitlement of up to **one usual working week in each 12 months**, total across dependants, to provide or arrange care. A qualifying dependant relies on the employee and has care needs due to old age, a disability, or illness/injury expected to require care for more than three months. Leave can be half days, days or a week. Notice applies, and serious business disruption can allow postponement under the published rules. Employees need not provide evidence of the dependant's care needs. Do not call this paid leave or one week for each dependant.

**Carer's assessment:** [GOV.UK Care and support statutory guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/care-act-statutory-guidance/care-and-support-statutory-guidance), paragraphs 6.16–6.19, requires assessment where someone provides/intends to provide care for another adult and appears to have support needs. The assessment considers caring's effect on wellbeing and work; an assessment is not a promise of a service or cash award. [GOV.UK national explanation](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/care-and-support-whats-changing/care-and-support-whats-changing), section 3.2, directs a carer to the council where the cared-for person lives.

[Bolton Council, Carers information and services](https://www.bolton.gov.uk/carers), “Carers' assessments”, publishes the route to request an assessment: Early Intervention Team, **01204 337860**, **EITTeam@bolton.gov.uk**. Use this as a request route and link to the council for the appropriate assessment, including different arrangements for young carers or caring for children. The council also links to Bolton Carers Support. No appointment, response time or resulting support is guaranteed here.

**Carer's Allowance:** [GOV.UK eligibility](https://www.gov.uk/carers-allowance/eligibility) confirms that eligibility depends on both people and the care provided: at least 35 hours' care each week, a qualifying benefit for the person cared for, age 16+, earnings, education, residence and immigration conditions. At this check the earnings ceiling is £204 a week after specified deductions; avoid hard-coding rates in a discussion plan. Scotland uses Carer Support Payment. Link to the current eligibility check rather than telling an employee they qualify.

[GOV.UK effect on other benefits](https://www.gov.uk/carers-allowance/effect-on-other-benefits) warns that claiming can affect benefits for **both** the carer and the person receiving care. The cared-for person's severe disability additions may stop; Universal Credit can be reduced by the Carer's Allowance amount. Encourage a benefits check before claiming; do not present the allowance as automatic additional income.

## Disability duty and the employer's private comparison

[Acas, What reasonable adjustments are](https://www.acas.org.uk/reasonable-adjustments) explains that employers must make reasonable adjustments for disabled people when they know, or reasonably could know, about the disability. Reasonableness depends on the circumstances, effectiveness, practicality, affordability and safety. Employers pay for reasonable adjustments; Access to Work does not replace that duty. The prototype does not determine whether a condition meets the legal definition.

The disability adjustment duty is for the disabled worker's disadvantage. A non-disabled carer does not gain that duty solely through caring. Carers can still have the employment rights above and discrimination protections: [Acas types of disability discrimination](https://www.acas.org.uk/disability-discrimination/types-of-disability-discrimination) includes less favourable treatment because of a connection to a disabled person. Do not imply carers lack all discrimination protection. The statutory adjustment duty is framed around disadvantage to a disabled person in [Equality Act 2010, section 20](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/section/20); the direct statute fetch returned an unsupported-content error during this check, so the readable Acas explanation supplies the operational guidance.

[GOV.UK, Being taken to an employment tribunal: if you lose](https://www.gov.uk/being-taken-to-employment-tribunal-by-employee/lose-the-case), “Pay compensation”, confirms that discrimination compensation has **no limit**. This does not mean a claim will succeed or establish an expected award. Do not assign an AI-generated legal-risk value or probability.

Suitable fixed notice, authored from these sources: **“If a condition may be a disability, check your legal duty to make reasonable adjustments. Dismissing someone because of their disability risks a discrimination claim. Compensation for discrimination has no statutory cap.”**

[Acas, Capability and performance when someone is disabled](https://www.acas.org.uk/supporting-disabled-people/capability-and-performance-when-someone-is-disabled) requires all reasonable support to be considered and a full, fair process before further action. The tool should direct an affordability concern to Acas and discussion of alternatives/support. A comparison of employer-entered costs does not decide whether an adjustment is reasonable and must never recommend dismissal.

## Crisis routes

[NHS, Where to get urgent help for mental health](https://www.nhs.uk/nhs-services/mental-health-services/where-to-get-urgent-help-for-mental-health/) directs urgent mental-health needs to **111**, selecting the mental-health option. It directs a life at risk or inability to keep someone safe to **999 or A&E**. It lists **Samaritans 116 123** as a listening service. Its displayed review date is 26 April 2023 and next review was due April 2026; the page remains published, and the phone service is independently confirmed below.

[Samaritans, Talk to us on the phone](https://www.samaritans.org/how-we-can-help/contact-samaritan/talk-us-phone/) confirms **116 123** is free, available day and night. Listening support does not replace emergency response. ADJUST should stop the case flow on a possible crisis indication and show these routes without claiming a clinical safety assessment.

## Occupational health and local employment support

[Acas, Using occupational health at work](https://www.acas.org.uk/using-occupational-health-at-work) explains its role in work barriers, adjustments and returning after sickness absence. Employers may have an internal service or use an outside agency. Check the organisation's policy and involve the employee. Permission is required before an employer contacts the worker's doctor; do not collect medical reports in ADJUST.

[WorkWell Bolton's published page](https://letskeepboltonmoving.co.uk/education-employment/workwell/) describes health/employment support and displays a referral form. [Bolton Council's launch notice](https://www.bolton.gov.uk/news/article/1838/workwell-is-just-the-job-for-bolton), dated 15 January 2025, says the local service was funded until March 2026. A working webpage does not confirm October 2026 funding, capacity or current referral acceptance. Retain **“Confirm current availability”** and avoid a promise of an appointment or support.

## Implementation boundaries

- These citations support general signposting; the demo uses fictional examples only.
- Private descriptions, diagnosis names and medical details must never enter a shared handover or printable plan. Reviewed work needs and employer practical constraints are the shared material.
- Closing a page can clear the active in-memory draft, but an exported PDF or copied handover link cannot be recalled. Avoid absolute claims that ADJUST erases copies held elsewhere.
- This file does not add material to the runtime source catalogue or mark any service availability as confirmed.
